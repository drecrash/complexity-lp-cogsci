## Background

My eight-week summer research program at Ohio State made me realize how interesting research can be, and also opened my eyes to how little I really do know about the fields I'm interested in pursuing.

So I made a rough six-week long "curriculum" for me to follow and get up to speed on these topics before I start my first semester at UCSB.

There was a lot I wanted to learn: game theory, statistical mechanics, graph theory, neural networks, systems theory ... the list could go on for a while. Unfortunately, I am but a human, and it came to my attention that it would be more effective to focus on a few subjects rather than just scrape the surface of all of them.

So I settled on three core fields: **complex systems, linear optimization, and computational cognitive science**

---

## Plan

**Complexity and LP**

I've chosen textbooks for studying complex systems and linear optimization.

Each week, I will complete some number chapters or sections from each textbook, take notes, and work through their exercises. More importantly, I will also be implementing 1 - 2 programming projects based on the material from either or both textbooks.

The point is less to improve programming ability, and more to make sure I know what the concepts in each chapter actually represent realistically.

**Cognitive Science**

For cognitive science (CS), I'm taking a less structured approach. Every week, I'll try to read, annotate, and summarize 3-7 research papers or academic articles on CS principles and sub-fields. <br>
*Currently unsure of whether or not to publish these annotations and summaries*
